## 1.9.0 — 2026-08-30 — THE THEMATIC SET (bitcrush / snesverb / tape) + two bug fixes

Ray's ear passed the 1.8.0 suite verbatim ("all the effects sound great, pass for me")
and asked for more thematic ones — "bit crushing might be useful, snes style reverb,
tape simulation etc". This is those three, on the same table-driven grain, plus two
proposals offered for a ruling, plus the two kit bugs banked from the shell-score lane.

### Does this re-ear-gate anything?

**The new effects: no.** Defaults are OFF and the additions are inert until a score
declares them, exactly as in 1.8.0.

**The bug fixes: YES, for two narrow classes — see "Golden impact" below.** One shipped
golden changed, and it changed by APPENDING a cut-off tail without altering a single
previously-rendered frame (proven at the byte level, and asserted by a standing test).

### The three effects

- **`bitcrush`** — bit-depth quantiser plus a zero-order-hold decimator, independently
  controlled (`bits`, `rate` in Hz, `wet`). Presets `gentle` / `chip` / `destroy`.
  The quantiser is a `WaveShaperNode` on a staircase curve, which reproduces a
  quantiser EXACTLY rather than approximating it: measured output lattice spacing at
  4/6/8 bits is 1.2500e-1 / 3.1250e-2 / 7.8130e-3 against an ideal 2/2^bits, with
  98.4–98.7 % of samples landing on the lattice. The hold is an N-tap rectangular FIR
  on a `ConvolverNode` — literally the zero-order-hold magnitude response, nulling at
  multiples of sampleRate/N (measured 250–1000× below the passband).
  **Stated limit:** it does not model sample-and-hold ALIASING. See "Findings" below.
- **`snesverb`** — the SNES DSP's echo: a short ring buffer whose **feedback path runs
  through the hardware's 8-tap FIR**. That filter is the character; a plain feedback
  delay is a delay, but one that re-filters every pass compounds the same loss each
  time, which is why a SNES echo sounds like a SNES echo. `edl` is the hardware's
  16 ms delay step (0–15), `fir` takes `bypass` / `lowpass` / `bright` or eight signed
  bytes of your own, plus `feedback`, `wet`, and a `mono` collapse. Presets `cave` /
  `hall` / `slap` / `dry`. The FIR is an 8-sample convolver IR with normalisation off,
  so the taps ARE the hardware's bytes over 128.
- **`tape`** — `wowRate`/`wowDepth`, `flutterRate`/`flutterDepth`, `drive`, `rolloff`,
  `hiss`, `wet`. Presets `studio` / `worn` / `ruined`. Wow and flutter are separate
  LFOs into one delay line, so each contributes a peak pitch deviation of exactly
  `depth * 2*pi*rate` and each is independently measurable by zeroing the other.
  Saturation is a `tanh` waveshaper — **normalised by `drive`, not by `tanh(drive)`**,
  so its small-signal gain is 1 and turning drive up saturates instead of getting
  louder (the textbook normalisation quietly added ~19 dB at `drive: 9` and clipped the
  first demo render at 1.725).

  **THE HISS LAW.** `hiss` is 0 in every preset and in the fallback, is capped at
  `FX_MAX_HISS` (0.05), and **builds no nodes at all when zero**. A fully declared tape
  effect running over a silent track renders exactly zero samples — asserted per-frame
  by test, not by inspection. Noise is something a score chooses, never a floor it
  inherits.

### Two proposals — built, demoed, adopted by nobody

Offered for Ray's ruling; no score declares them and they cost nothing while undeclared.

- **`spring`** — a spring tank: a Schroeder allpass cascade (dispersion — the boing)
  into a damped IR. `stages`, `spread`, `seconds`, `decay`, `wet`.
- **`ringmod`** — four-quadrant ring modulation via an oscillator driving a gain (an
  `AudioParam` gain goes negative, so this is a true multiply, not tremolo). The input
  is replaced by sidebands at |f − carrier| and f + carrier with the carrier suppressed
  ~2000:1, measured.

Vinyl crackle was considered and **declined**: it is a noise bed by definition, and the
kit already has exactly one sanctioned way to put noise in a score — tape `hiss`, opt-in
and capped. A second, less bounded one works against the HISS LAW rather than with it.

### Bug fix (a) — chip voices now render their release

`chipPulse` and `chipNoise` ignored their release envelope. Both build a per-segment
gate on top of the ADSR, and both closed that gate at the wrong moment:

- **`chipPulse`** closed the final segment at `t + dur` — where the SUSTAIN ends. The
  envelope's release then ran from `t+dur` to `t+dur+r` against a gate already pinned at
  EPS, so the release was rendered at −80 dB. Measured before the fix: a 32× release
  sweep (0.05 s → 1.6 s) moved the audible end of the note by **0.0000 s**, flat at
  0.4388 s at every value.
- **`chipNoise`** was worse and the banked report understated it. Each source's gate
  closed at the NEXT TICK rather than at the next source, so a note with no retriggers
  sounded for **one tick — 20 ms** and then sat at EPS for the rest of its length,
  release included. Measured before the fix: a 0.4 s note ended **0.0588 s** in.
- **`chipTriangle` was never affected** — it has no segment gate — and its measured
  behaviour is byte-for-byte unchanged. The 1.9.0 tests pin its four release-sweep
  values as the control that proves the fix was targeted.

The gate now holds open to `stopTime` on the final segment/source, so the ADSR owns the
note's end, exactly as it always did for `chipTriangle`.

### Bug fix (b) — the render budget respects the score's real tail

The offline buffer was budgeted at a CONSTANT — `RETIRE_TAIL` (0.75 s) plus the reverb
IR, 1.45 s in total — regardless of what the score actually played. A score whose last
note rang longer than that had it guillotined by the end of the file.

`voiceTailSeconds()` (new, exported, pure) is now the single source of truth for how
long each voice sounds, mirroring each one's own envelope; `specTailOverhangSeconds()`
uses it to measure how far a score rings past its last bar; the renderer reserves
**`max(historical budget, real overhang)`**. The max() is what keeps this safe: a score
that already fitted cannot move, so its length and its bytes are unchanged.

**Section boundaries are now a CHOICE, and the default did not move.** A note still
sounding when the next section arrives is cut by the 80 ms crossfade — measured, a bell
with a 1.2 s release ends at 2.069 s against a boundary at 2.000 s. That is a deliberate
design (it stops the outgoing track bleeding into the incoming one), so changing the
default would silently re-render every multi-section score in the fleet and force a
re-ear-gate for a taste change nobody asked for. Instead: `retireMode: 'crossfade'`
(default, identical to 1.8.0) or `'tail'` with a `retireHold`, declarable per score as
`spec.retire`. Declaring the default explicitly renders byte-identically to omitting it.

**The one audio golden that changed, changed additively.** `examples/demo-spec.json`
holds a pad with `dur: 3.5` and `r: 0.25` — a 3.75 s tail — against a 15.0 s score, so

its final 10 ms at −118.8 dB. **The first 725 445 frames are bit-identical to the whole

Nothing that was audible before has changed — the render simply no longer stops

or `chipNoise`, or whose last note was being truncated, wants a re-render and a
re-ear-gate. The kit does not decide that.

### Findings worth carrying

- **`disableNormalization: true` is IGNORED by node-web-audio-api.** Probed: a
  `PeriodicWave` with a single harmonic of amplitude 2 renders at peak 1.0, with the
  flag both true and false. Browsers honour it per spec. This ruled out the comb-gate
  decimator (its image level would differ between the renderer and the shipped game —
  the exact cross-engine divergence the determinism law forbids), and it means the
  kit's existing `makePulseWave` duty-cycle waves are peak-normalised in the offline
  renderer and *not* in a browser. **Not changed here** — it is outside this lane's
  scope and touching it would alter every chip render — but it is a real latent
  divergence in a kit whose central law is "same seed, same score", and it deserves its
  own decision.
- **Nested feedback cycles render as silence** in node-web-audio-api. A cycle enclosing
  the allpass sections' own internal cycles gives exactly 0.0 output, with or without a
  convolver in the loop, even though every cycle contains a `DelayNode`. The kit's
  `delay` effect is unaffected because its cycle is flat. This is why `spring` ships
  with no feedback parameter.
- **Per-voice send AMOUNTS were unbounded for any effect after the first three.** 1.8.0
  promised in a comment that a new effect gets its send validated "by appearing in
  `FX_KINDS`", but `OPTION_RANGES` listed `verb`/`delay`/`chorus` by hand. Now derived
  from the table, so the promise is true.

## 1.8.0 — 2026-08-29 — THE FX SUITE (reverb / delay / chorus)

**No shipped score's audio changes.** Additive only, defaults OFF: with no `fx`
declared, `createBand` builds exactly the 1.7.1 graph and every existing render stays
**byte-identical** across 1.7.1 → 1.8.0. Proof, not assertion — the golden fixtures
(`m1-hardening` golden render, `m3-samples` legacy + sample renders, the API replay mix
and all three stems) keep their hashes; only `api-t1-replay-output.json`'s
`scoreArtifactSha256` moves, because a persisted score pins `kitVersion` in its JSON.
**The re-ear-gate rule does NOT fire.** It fires the moment a score declares `fx`,
because that score's audio is new.

The fleet's scores had no effects at all: one fixed send reverb with two knobs
(`seconds`, `decay`) and no delay or chorus anywhere. This adds the three workhorses,
built to survive the determinism law rather than bolted on.

- **Reverb** — a generated-IR convolver with three characters as presets: `room`,
  `hall`, `plate`. Each carries `damping` (tail HF cutoff), `preDelay` (seconds before
  the tail starts) and `wet`. The IR is synthesised from the band's seeded mulberry32
  and damped by an **arithmetic one-pole whose cutoff falls across the tail**, so highs
  die before lows — the thing "damping" actually means. Declaring `fx.reverb`
  **replaces** the legacy send reverb rather than running a second one, so a score's
  existing `verb:` amounts land in the upgraded room unchanged.
- **Delay** — feedback delay with tempo-synced beat fractions (`1/2`, `1/4.`, `1/4`,
  `1/4t`, `1/8.`, `1/8`, `1/8t`, `1/16`) resolved against `fx.bpm`, a raw `time`
  override, `feedback`, a lowpass `damping` **inside the loop** so successive repeats
  darken, `wet`, and a **stereo ping-pong** variant. Presets: `slap`, `eighth`,
  `dotted`, `quarter`.
- **Chorus** — an ensemble of LFO-modulated delay lines (`voices`, `rate`, `depth`,
  `spread`, `width`, `wet`) panned across the field. Presets: `subtle`, `lush`.
  Alternate voices take an inverted modulation gain, which is a deterministic
  180-degree phase offset without needing to start an oscillator before time zero.
- **Architecture.** Effects are declared once on the band and fed by **per-voice
  sends** named exactly the way `verb` already is — `delay:` and `chorus:`, both 0..1,
  on every voice that has a wet tap (`kick` has none in the kit and gains none here).
  Each track bus owns its own send gain per effect, ramped with the track, so a scene
  change cannot bleed the outgoing track's wet back in. `fx.master` additionally
  inserts any effect across the whole sum with its own `wet`/`dry`.
- **Determinism is the design constraint.** Double-render byte-equality is asserted
  with reverb + delay + chorus + a master insert all engaged. IRs come from the seeded
  PRNG (a different seed gives a different room; the same seed gives the same one);
  no wall-clock and no `Math.random` enters the fx path.
- **Renders reserve their own tail.** `fxTailSeconds()` extends the offline buffer to
  hold a declared reverb IR or echo train, and returns **0** when no fx are declared,
  so historical render lengths cannot move.
- **Extensibility.** Kinds live in `FX_KINDS`, builders in `FX_BUILDERS`, send names in
  `FX_VOICE_SEND`. A later phaser/flanger/width is one more entry in each plus a preset
  row — no signature change and no API break. Deliberately NOT built now.
- **Proof:** `proof/fx-suite-20260829/` — one phrase, five treatments, identical notes
  and sends throughout (`node scripts/fx-demo.mjs`). Measured against the dry control
  in the window where the control is silent: reverb +40 dB, delay +42 dB, chorus and
  ping-pong both decorrelating the channels. Tests: `test/kit-1.8.0-fx.test.mjs`.

### Adoption

Scores adopt the suite **per game, at their next natural re-render** — this round wires
nothing into any game. A score that declares `fx` is new audio and therefore
**re-ear-gates under that game's own rules** (fleet re-ear-gate rules apply per game);
a score that declares none is untouched and needs nothing.

### Migration notes for embedded ports

1. **Nothing is required.** A port that declares no `fx` renders identically.
2. To use the suite in an embedded build, re-sync `band.js` and pass `fx` to
   `createBand`; the whole FX SUITE block is self-contained and depends only on
   `mulberry32` and the standard WebAudio node set (`ConvolverNode`, `DelayNode`,
   `BiquadFilterNode`, `OscillatorNode`, `StereoPannerNode`). Each builder degrades to
   `null` on a context lacking its nodes rather than throwing.
3. 1.7.1 remains in the score-compatibility matrix.

## 1.7.1 — 2026-08-29 — sub opt-out, drone HP, per-event nudge, LF-overload probe

**No shipped score's audio changes.** Additive only: defaults are unchanged, so every
existing render that does not opt into the new fields stays **byte-identical** across
1.7.0 → 1.7.1. Golden fixtures (`m1-hardening`, `m3-samples`, API replay mix + stems)
must keep their output hashes; only the persisted score artifact hash moves when a
score pins the new `kitVersion`. **The re-ear-gate rule does NOT fire.**

- **`sub: 0` / `sub: false` on `pad`, `drone`, and `bass`.** Disables the hard-wired
  sub-octave oscillator (pad/drone: `freq*0.5` at gain 0.7; bass: forced sine at
  `freq*0.5` / 0.6). Absent or any other value keeps the historical sub **on**.
- **`hp:<Hz>` on `drone`.** Optional high-pass before the lowpass. Default off (no HP
  node when the option is absent).
- **`nudge:<ms>` per event.** Small positive/negative onset offset applied at schedule
  time (top-level step field or `opts.nudge`). Default 0. Range ±50 ms (same band as
  performance humanize timing).

## 1.7.0 — 2026-08-29 — the pitched sampler voice

**No shipped score's audio changes.** This release is purely additive: it adds new
exports to `band.js` and a new opt-in binding shape. Proof, not assertion — the golden
render fixtures (`m1-hardening`, `m3-samples`, the API replay mix and all three stems)
are byte-identical across the 1.6.0 → 1.7.0 boundary; only the persisted score
artifact's own hash moves, because a score pins `kitVersion` in its JSON. **The
re-ear-gate rule therefore does NOT fire here.** It fires the moment a score opts into
a sampler voice, because that score's audio is new.

- **New:** a pitched multisample voice. Ray's endorsed sample families
  (`docs/RAY-SAMPLES-TIMBRE-CATALOG-2026-08-29.md` — MS-20, INSTRUMENTS, HUMBUCKER MF,
  From Mars) were all unreachable before this: the renderer copied sample frames 1:1
  with no rate conversion, the plan never read a note for a sample voice, and the pack
  format rejected any non-drum asset. All three blocks are gone.
- **Kit (`band.js`), four additions:** `SEMITONE_RATIO_Q16` + `midiToFrequency` +
  `noteToMidi` + `nearestMidi` (exact pitch arithmetic), `samplerRateQ16` /
  `samplerRateQ16FromFrequency`, `selectSamplerZone`, and `mixSamplerVoiceQ16` — a Q16
  fixed-point interpolating resampler with an integer gate/release envelope.
- **Determinism is the design constraint, not a nice-to-have.** The twelve semitone
  ratios are **hardcoded Q16 integers**, not `Math.pow(2, n/12)` computed at load:
  `Math.pow` is implementation defined, and ratio 11 lands 0.006 from a rounding
  boundary. Every operation on the audio path is integer arithmetic inside 2^53.
  Pinned by a fixed-vector regression hash and a double-render byte-equality test.
- **Pack format 1.1.0** (`sample-pack.mjs`): `rootNote`, `rootNoteSource`,
  `measuredF0Millihertz`, and the `sampler` voice class. 1.0.0 packs stay valid and
  byte-frozen. Details: `docs/M3-PACK-FORMAT.md`.
- **The builder measures pitch and distrusts filenames.** `tools/pitch.mjs` (YIN —
  arithmetic only, so a pack build is reproducible across machines) measures f0 and
  overrides a disagreeing filename label, reporting every disagreement. Building the
  demo pack immediately caught **three**: every `INSTRUMENTS/SNES RHODES` file is
  labelled an octave above its actual pitch. It also resolved the catalog's flagged
  `HUMBUCKER MF-044` — the label is right; the catalog's harmonic-product estimate was
  two octaves high on a note whose fundamental is weak.
- **Score schema:** `sampleVoices.<voice>` accepts `{ kind: 'sampler', zones: [...],
  gain, release }` on `pad | drone | bell | pluck | bass | lead`. Chip voices are
  deliberately excluded — a sampled `chipPulse` would be a lie about what the score is
  doing. One-shot bindings stay drum-only: a one-shot has no root, so on a pitched lane
  it would sound one fixed pitch for every note. A sampler note that falls through the
  zone map is a validation error, not silence.
- **Proof:** `sample-packs/sampler-demo-17` (8 assets, 999.5 kB) and
  `examples/sampler-demo-spec.json`, rendered to `proof/sampler-17-20260829/` as a full
  mix plus one stem per sampled voice.

### Migration notes for embedded ports

1. **Nothing is required.** A port that plays no sampler voice needs no change; the
   existing voices are untouched and render identically.
2. **To play sampler voices in an embedded build**, copy the whole SAMPLER CORE block
   out of `band.js` (it is self-contained and depends on nothing else in the file) and
   drive `mixSamplerVoiceQ16` into an `Int32Array` bus. Do NOT reach for
   `AudioBufferSourceNode.playbackRate` instead: it is float-rate and would break
   byte-identical replay against the custody render that certified the score.
3. **Ports must carry the assets too.** A sampler score is only reproducible with its
   exact pack; the score pins `manifestSha256` and `contentSha256`, and every zone pins
   its asset's `pcmSha256`. Budget the bytes — 88.2 kB per second of canonical audio.
4. `KIT_VERSION.sha` changed, so any embedded copy of `band.js` must be re-synced and
   any locally pinned kit hash updated. 1.6.0 remains in the score-compatibility matrix,
   so existing scores keep validating untouched.
